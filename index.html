EasyShare privacy policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

EasyShare sends files from your phone directly to a phone near you. It is built so that your files and your information stay between you and the person you send to.

What EasyShare does not do

  • No servers. EasyShare has no servers. Files go straight from one phone to the other over a private Wi-Fi link the two phones make, or over a Wi-Fi network you are both on. They never go over the internet or mobile data.
  • No accounts, no sign-in.
  • No tracking, no ads, no analytics, no crash reports. Nothing about you or your use of the app is sent to us or to anyone else.
  • No location. On Android 10–12, Android lists Wi-Fi Direct and Bluetooth as location features and asks for the location permission. EasyShare uses that permission only to make the private Wi-Fi link and to find nearby phones. It never reads, stores or sends your location.
  • No access to your photos or files beyond the ones you pick to send.

What stays on your phone

  • A device key, made the first time you open EasyShare. It proves to phones you've paired with that it's really you. It never leaves your phone and isn't backed up.
  • Your paired devices: the names of phones you've sent to or received from, so you don't need a code next time. Stored encrypted, on your phone only. You can forget any of them under Paired devices.
  • Files you receive, saved in Downloads/EasyShare.

Uninstalling EasyShare deletes all of this except the files you received.

What nearby phones can see

  • While EasyShare can receive, your phone announces itself over Bluetooth with an anonymous tag that changes every 15 minutes. Paired phones recognise it. With your name hidden (below), other phones can't tell it's you.
  • Your phone's name (for example "Galaxy M21") is included so people can pick you. Turn off "Show my name to everyone nearby" and only paired phones see your name; others see "EasyShare phone".
  • When you send, the other phone sees your phone's name and the names and sizes of the files, before it accepts. Files are encrypted on the way (TLS 1.3 with post-quantum key exchange); only the two phones can read them.
  • First time with a new phone, a 6-digit code on the receiving phone is typed on the sending phone, so a stranger nearby can't pretend to be someone else.

Receiving with EasyShare closed

If "Receive when EasyShare is closed" is on, EasyShare keeps a quiet "Ready to receive" notification and keeps announcing itself as above, so nearby phones can send to you. Nothing is accepted without you tapping Accept. Turn it off in Paired devices, or with "Turn off" in the notification.

Sending to a phone without EasyShare

If you choose "No app on their phone? Show a code", your phone makes a private Wi-Fi network and serves the files you picked to the other phone's web browser. Only phones that join that network with the code can download them. It closes by itself shortly after the download.

Children

EasyShare collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
